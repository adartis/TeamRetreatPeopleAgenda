Straw man 1 · Time on BD

BD: A little every day, or all at once?

A little every day

Gives
  • Constant BD growth: skills build steadily
  • Light, frequent coaching touchpoints
  • A good fit for ongoing pipeline work
Costs
  • A daily crunch against client deadlines
  • Constant switching between mindsets
  • Hard for country directors to see or protect

All at once, once a year

Gives
  • Deep immersion in a full bid
  • Client work protected most of the year
  • Easy to plan around, once scheduled
Costs
  • Skills can fade before the next burst
  • A long absence from the client
  • Misses opportunities outside the window

Straw man 1 · What each kind of work teaches

Client work and BD teach different things

Client work builds

  • Deep knowledge of one client and its politics
  • Solving real problems under real constraints
  • Day-to-day relationships and expectations
  • Delivering to scope and budget
  • Quality, consistency and follow-through

BD builds

  • Reading a new client's needs quickly
  • Framing problems before work begins
  • Persuasive writing and pitching
  • Scoping, pricing and choosing which work to pursue
  • Working across countries and disciplines, at speed
The link is coaching: they complement each other only if a senior colleague consciously draws out the connection.

Straw man 2 · Stocktake

Development is driven by the work we do

What this asks of us

If we commit to any of this as an institution, managing the trade-offs is our job, not the junior colleague's.

1

The work shapes the learning

What colleagues learn depends on the work we give them and how we lead it.

2

Commitment puts the onus on us

A share for BD, a rhythm, remote support: each needs leadership to hold the trade-offs.

3

Contract clearly and predictably

Who has a colleague's time, for how long, and with what expectations, agreed in advance.

4

Beware overcommitment

Colleagues pulled in too many directions cope rather than grow, or burn out. So can we.
